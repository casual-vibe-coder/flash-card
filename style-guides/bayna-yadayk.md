# Style guide: العربية بين يديك (Al-Arabiyya Bayna Yadayk)

A four-book Modern Standard Arabic course (Madinah Islamic University) for adult Muslim learners, A1 to advanced. All instructions and prose are Arabic only.
Pick the level that matches the target learner, then follow that Level section. Do not mix levels.

## Quick summary
Neutral, polite, wholesome Modern Standard Arabic with an openly Muslim worldview. No dialect, slang, romance or humour. Sentences grow by level: about 3 words (Book 1), 5-8 (Book 2), 15-25 (Book 3), 20-30 (Book 4). Beginner texts are strict question/answer dialogues and 5-8 sentence factual paragraphs, fully vowelled. Higher levels move to expository essays, anecdotes and Qur'an/hadith proof-texts, with thinner vowelling in Books 3-4. Names are ordinary Muslim names. Vocabulary is recycled across dialogue, reading and questions. Texts end with a plain fact, moral or blessing, then comprehension questions.

## Voice and register
- Textbook-neutral MSA. Courtesy formulas are normal: السلام عليكم، الحمد لله، إن شاء الله، جزاك الله خيراً، شفاك الله.
- Third-person, present tense, matter-of-fact. No narrator personality, no suspense, no irony. The tone is teacherly but not preachy.
- Religion is woven into daily life (prayer, mosque, Qur'an, Hajj). Write ﷺ after the Prophet's name and رضي الله عنه after Companions. Quote Qur'an in ﴿ ﴾ with [surah: verse], fully vowelled.
- Books 3-4 are didactic and exhortative (essays, advice, Companion anecdotes). Book 4 can address the reader directly.
- Use Arabic-Indic digits (١ ٢ ٣) for numbering.

## Devices and patterns
- **Dialogue openers:** a greeting (السلام عليكم / وعليكم السلام) or a direct question (من أين حضرت؟ أمريض أنت؟).
- **Dialogue closers:** a blessing or thanks (مع السلامة، شكراً، شفاك الله، يا لها من نصيحة طيبة!).
- **Dialogue format:** name, colon, line, one turn per line, strict A-B alternation. The answer echoes the question's key word. Role labels (المستأجر، الزوج) are used when anonymous, and ط١ / ط٢ in drills. There are no quote marks or "he said" tags. Anecdotes use "قال القاضي:" with no quote marks.
- **Reading openers:** Book 1 starts with a name plus a fact (شريف طالب في الجامعة). Book 2 starts with a general truth or a colon definition. Books 3-4 start with a definition, a hadith attribution, or a narrator frame (وصّى أب ابنته فقال:).
- **Connectors by level:** Book 1 mostly و and ثم. Book 2 adds لأنّ، لكنّ، عندما، بعد أن، لذلك، أما...فـ. Book 3 adds إذ، حيث، بينما، لذا، وقد، أي. Book 4 adds لأنّ، ثمّ، حتى، بل، مما، على أنّ، وفي الحقيقة.
- **Recycling:** one frame repeated with changed nouns (هذا عمّه... / هذا ابنه...). Target words return in questions, true/false items and the closing paragraph. Names and settings stay fixed within a unit.
- **Close with exercises:** ما/لماذا/متى/كم questions, ✓/× with correction, fill from a box, order the sentences.

## Never / avoid
- Never describe or depict faces or eyes in any image-prompt or scene-note text. Describe scenes by setting, clothing and action only.
- No dialect, slang, English or transliteration inside Arabic text.
- No romance or dating (marriage appears only as advice or a plain fact), no alcohol, no music, no pork, no irreverent or pun-based humour. Humour appears only in the Book 3 anecdote unit.
- No invented Qur'an or hadith, and never unvowelled Qur'an.
- No poetry, proverbs, idioms or literary ornament in Books 1-2.
- No long monologues, plot twists or story arcs below Book 4.
- No sentence or paragraph longer than the level allows. Do not use Book 3 grammar in a Book 1 text.
- Mixed-gender chat is rare. Dialogues are same-gender except husband/wife, family, host/guest, hospital or work roles.

## Level 1: Book 1 (beginner)
- **Sentence length:** min 1-2, typical 3-5, max about 10-14 (only in late-unit answers). Units 1-2 are 2-4 words ("هذا أخي"). Dialogue turns are 3-9 words.
- **Paragraphs:** 5-8 sentences, about 25-70 words, never over about 75. Clauses chain with و. Dialogues run 4-8 turn pairs.
- **Tashkeel:** full on every word, including case endings, shadda, sukun and tanween, even in late units. Lighter only in tests, gap-fill drills and the word list.
- **Scenarios and cast:** greetings, family, renting a flat, daily routine, food, prayer, study, jobs, shopping, weather, hobbies, travel, Hajj, hospital, Eid. Cast: أحمد، بدر، ندى، هدى، عمر، عثمان، فاطمة، طارق، ليث، ثامر، سعيد.
- **Grammar:** هذا/هذه، هو/هي, nominal sentences, هل/ما/من/أين/كم/متى, possessive suffixes, numbers 1-15, present tense, سـ future, simple past from about Unit 7, adjective agreement.
- **Vocabulary:** greetings, nationalities, professions, family, house, food, prayer, clock times, riyal prices, weather, health, Hajj rites, Hijri months.
- **Samples:**
  - أَحْمَد : هَذَا أَخِي . هُوَ مُدَرِّس .
  - المُؤَجِّر : فِي الشَّقَّةِ خَمْسُ غُرَفٍ .
  - الزَّوْجُ : أَنَا جَوْعَانُ جِدّاً .
  - ثَامِر : حَضَرْتُ مِنَ اليَمَنِ . أَنَا يَمَنِيٌّ .

## Level 2: Book 2
- **Sentence length:** dialogue min 2, typical 3-7 (median about 5-6), max about 11. Narrative typically 6-10. Exposition typically 8-13, up to about 15-20 in lists. Hard cap about 20.
- **Paragraphs:** 5-8 sentences; a whole passage is about 140-220 words. Dialogues run 14-20 turns. Passages open with a title question and 4 pre-reading questions (تهيئة).
- **Tashkeel:** nearly full on dialogues and passages, with case endings. Exercise lines are lighter from Unit 9 on. Qur'an is always full.
- **Scenarios and cast:** a friend visiting a sick friend, a doctor, married life, city vs village, globalisation, cleanliness, a convert to Islam (كارلوس), youth, security, pollution, energy. Cast: عامر، حازم، هشام، إبراهيم، فاطمة، زينب، بلال.
- **Grammar:** لم + jussive, لا الناهية, أن + subjunctive, كان, imperative, نداء, حال, الذي/التي, passive in passing, conditional لولا/إذا.
- **Vocabulary:** about 60 new words per unit: health and food, recreation, marriage, city problems, internet and TV, Islam, crime, pollution, solar energy.
- **Samples:**
  - لا، لَمْ أَذْهَبْ إِلى الطَّبِيبِ، وَلَمْ أَتَنَاوَلْ أَيَّ دَواءٍ.
  - الحَمْدُ للهِ على سَلامَتِكَ يا إِبْراهِيمُ.
  - يَعيشُ مَلايينُ النّاسِ في المُدُنِ الكَبيرَةِ.
  - هُناكَ صُوَرٌ عَديدَةٌ لِتَلَوُّثِ البيئَةِ.

## Level 3: Book 3
- **Sentence length:** grammar examples 2-8 (typical 3-5). Exercise sentences 4-14 (typical 6-10). Expository and listening prose min about 6, typical 15-25, max about 35-40. Anecdote dialogue lines 3-12.
- **Paragraphs:** 2-4 paragraphs of about 40-120 words, often numbered (١ ٢ ٣). Whole texts run about 150-300 words. Writing tasks cap at 50, 150 or 200 words.
- **Tashkeel:** moderate. Headings, grammar examples, Qur'an and hadith are fully vowelled. Prose keeps case endings, shadda and tanween but is thinner. Exercises and tests mark only the target words, about 40-70 percent of words. (Main reading texts were barely sampled, so treat this as approximate.)
- **Scenarios and cast:** almost no fictional cast. Historical and archetypal figures: النبي ﷺ، الصحابة، عمر، أُبيّ، ابن تيمية; أب وابنة, زوج, طبيب. Topics: Qur'an, a Muslim youth's day, minorities, Sunnah, children and reading, brain drain, sleep, justice, animals, proverbs, marital disputes, parents and children, water, a father's advice.
- **Grammar:** إنّ وأخواتها, كان وأخواتها, أنواع الخبر, أدوات الشرط الجازمة and fa- in the answer, ثلاثة مفاعيل, البدل, التوكيد, مصدر, اسم الفاعل/المفعول, passive, حال, تمييز, قد + past, بينما...إذا.
- **Vocabulary:** religious-legal and abstract social nouns. Almost no shopping or travel logistics.
- **Samples:**
  - الجنةُ تحتَ أقدامِ الأمهاتِ.
  - متى تأتِنا تجدْ خيراً.
  - النومُ فترةٌ مِن الراحةِ، لا يَشعرُ النائمُ فيها بما يَحدُثُ حَولَه.
  - وصَّى أبٌ ابنتَهُ ليلةَ الزواجِ فقالَ:

## Level 4: Book 4 (advanced)
- **Sentence length:** min about 4, typical 18-25 in essays and 20-30 in narrative, max about 40-55 (clause chains and lists). Dialogue lines 3-20 (typical 6-12). Grammar examples 4-8, exercise stems 6-12.
- **Paragraphs:** essay paragraphs 80-150 words (5-8 lines). Narrative paragraphs 100-170 words, numbered. Intensive texts run about 350-500 words, late wider-reading texts 600-800, listening scripts about 300-350. Texts include rhetorical questions and exclamations.
- **Tashkeel:** heavy to near-full in Part 1 (Units 1-8), with case endings. Narrative in Part 2 is lighter, mostly key words, shadda and tanween. Qur'an and grammar tables are always full. Exercise stems are mostly bare.
- **Scenarios and cast:** social essays (smoking, leisure, friendship, security, energy), the Hijra and Abyssinia, a judge and a stranger in court, a father's letters, a fisherman and a sage. Cast is mostly adult male: عائشة، أبو بكر، عمر، بلال، شريح; anonymous القاضي، الشيخ، الجار.
- **Grammar:** مبالغة and صفة مشبهة, عمل المصدر, اسم التفضيل, اسم الآلة, نعم/بئس, كاد وأخواتها, جمع القلة والكثرة, conditional, وقد + past, إنّ...لـ, long إضافة chains of 4+ nouns, relative clauses.
- **Vocabulary:** health and economics, holy cities with dates, legal terms, globalisation, ethics, tool nouns, crime and punishment, energy. No glosses; meaning is learned through exercises.
- **Samples:**
  - إِنَّ الْقُلُوبَ تَمَلُّ كَمَا تَمَلُّ الْأَبْدَانُ
  - قَالَ الْقَاضِي: هَاتِ دَلِيلَكَ.
  - عَسَى اللهُ أَنْ يَرْحَمَنَا.
  - مَا الْعَالَمُ إِلَّا بَحْرٌ زَاخِرٌ
