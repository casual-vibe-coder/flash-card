# Style guide: العربية بين يديك (Al-Arabiyya Bayna Yadayk)

Write Arabic reading passages and dialogues that feel like this textbook series (Madinah Islamic University / Arabic Language Institute, King Abdulaziz Endowment). Follow the rules below.

## 1. Voice and register
- Clean, neutral Modern Standard Arabic. No dialect, no slang, no internet speech.
- Tone: polite, wholesome, instructive. The text exists to teach, so it is never ironic, romantic, or edgy.
- The worldview is openly Muslim and the content is clean. Prayer, Qur'an, mosque, family, study and good manners appear naturally. Mention the Prophet with ﷺ. Quote Qur'an or hadith only at upper levels, in ﴿ ﴾ with the surah and verse reference.
- Speakers are real-sounding named people from many countries (Thabit, Layth, Tamer, Salman, Abu Bakr and Umar). Names are plain Arab-Muslim names.
- Never describe or depict faces or eyes in any illustration or scene note.

## 2. Themes, characters and settings by level
- **Book 1 (beginner):** self-introduction, nationality and country, family, profession (طالب، مدرّس، طبيب، مهندس)
  - Places: mosque, bathroom/wudu, room, house, university, library
  - Numbers, hobbies (reading, travel), likes and dislikes, daily routine
  - Dialogue partners are two students or a student and a teacher.
- **Book 2:** education and science, "the world is a small village" (rich and poor countries, globalisation), the Arabic language, prizes, cleanliness, Islam, youth
  - Settings: a university seminar, a family deciding what to study or whom to marry, a grandfather advising a grandson.
  - Dialogues compare opinions: "Do you agree that...? Why?"
- **Book 3:** a day in the life of a Muslim youth (duas on waking, entering the toilet, leaving the mosque), the everlasting miracle (the Qur'an)
  - Also Arabic proverbs and wisdom, travel, work, society.
  - Texts are expository and numbered into paragraphs. They are semi-formal essays with hadith quotations.
- **Book 4 (advanced):** Qur'anic stories (the Owner of the Two Gardens), Companions' biographies (Salman al-Farisi, Abu Bakr and Umar), the Prophet's seerah, moral dilemmas, theatre-style scenes
  - Narrative literary prose, with rhetorical questions and emotive description.

## 3. Sentence and paragraph shape
- **Words per sentence:** Book 1 has 2-6 words, with one idea per line. Book 2 has 6-12. Book 3 has 12-25, with an occasional long hadith-supported sentence. Book 4 has 15-40, with long chained narrative sentences.
- **Connectors:**
  - Book 1 uses almost none (و، هل، من أين).
  - Book 2 adds ثمّ، لأنّ، لذلك، لكنّ، عندما، قبل/بعد.
  - Book 3 adds إذا/إذا... فـ، حتّى، بعد أن، فإنّ، ولا سيّما.
  - Book 4 adds فـ، قد + الفعل, لقد, وقد, ولو, لأنّ, بينما, وهو + جملة حاليّة.
- **Dialogue format:** speaker name, then a colon, then the line, one turn per line.
  - Book 1 uses names (ليث: / ثامر:). Exercises use ط١ / ط٢ (Student 1 / Student 2).
  - Exchanges are strict question/answer pairs, with the answer repeating the question's key word: "هل ...؟ نعم، ..." / "ما ...؟ ...".
  - Book 4 can use stage-style scenes (مسرحيّة). Do not use quote marks or "he said" tags in beginner dialogue.
- **Recycling:** reuse each unit's target words 3-5 times across a passage. Follow the unit pattern that introduces a word in a dialogue, then a reading, then a comprehension question. Keep the same names and place across one unit. Keep new vocabulary to roughly 10-15 items per text, with the rest known.
- **Close each text with comprehension hooks.** Typical questions are ما / لماذا / متى / كم / بم, true/false with correction, and "what would you do?"

## 4. Grammar emphasis by level
- **Book 1:** pronouns, demonstratives (هذا/هذه), nominal sentences, هل / ما / مَن / أين / مِن, gender, numbers 1-10, the present tense of يَفعَل in singular forms, possessive suffixes, simple prepositions.
- **Book 2:** the past tense with all pronouns, past-to-present transformation, dual and plural (sound and broken), the five nouns, أن + subjunctive (يرغب في أن...), كان and its sisters, the comparative, the genitive construct (إضافة).
- **Book 3:** إنّ and its sisters, ظنّ and its sisters, كسا/أعطى (two-object verbs), الاستثناء بإلّا, case endings, حال, تمييز, conditional sentences.
- **Book 4:** rhetorical and morphological topics (the passive, derivatives, الأفعال الخمسة, التعجّب, الأسلوب), advanced syntax and narrative cohesion.

## 5. Tashkeel rules observed
- Book 1 and Book 2: **full diacritics on everything**, including dialogue and instructions. Show the ة and the tanween (كَبِيرَةٌ), shaddah, and sukun (مِنْ), and give case endings in Book 2.
- Book 3 and Book 4: reading passages are **fully vowelled in Book 4 narrative and in Book 3 headings and key terms**. Exercise sentences often have bare text, with vowels placed only on the target word or the cause of the question.
- Always vowel quoted Qur'an exactly, plus names that could be misread.
- Use ﷺ after the Prophet's name, and "عليه السلام" for other prophets. Use Arabic-Indic digits (١ ٢ ٣) for numbering.

## 6. DO / DON'T
**DO**
- Use one concrete situation per text, with a clear moral or fact at the end.
- Keep the vocabulary of the level and recycle it.
- Use diacritics consistently per the level.
- Use direct, simple, wholesome questions in dialogue.
- Use عَلَى / فِي / مِنْ and ordinary prepositions correctly.

**DON'T**
- Don't use dialect or modern slang.
- Don't use English or transliteration inside Arabic text.
- Don't use romance, violence, alcohol, music, or irreverent humour.
- Don't write literary ornament in Books 1-2.
- Don't invent hadith or Qur'an text, and don't give unvowelled Qur'an.
- Don't drift into long monologues at Books 1-2.
- Don't mix levels. A Book 1 text must not contain Book 3 grammar.

## 7. Few-shot anchors (as printed)
- Book 1: مِنْ أَيْنَ حَضَرْتَ؟ — حَضَرْتُ مِنَ اليَمَنِ. أَنَا يَمَنِيٌّ.
- Book 1: هَوايَتِي الرِّحْلاتُ وَالقِراءَةُ.
- Book 1: نَعَمْ، أَقْضِي العُطْلَةَ مَعَ الأُسْرَةِ فِي اليَمَنِ.
- Book 2: كانَتِ الأُمُّ تَدْعُو لِابْنِها بَشِيرٍ في صَلَواتِها بِالزَّواجِ.
- Book 2: وَالِدُكَ يَرْغَبُ في أَنْ تَدْرُسَ الطِّبَّ، وَأَنْتَ تَرْغَبُ في دِراسَةِ الهَنْدَسَةِ.
- Book 2: مَنْ قالَ إِنَّ الكِتابَ سَيَنْتَهِي قَرِيباً؟
- Book 3: إِنَّ اللهَ لَطيفٌ بِعِبادِهِ.
- Book 3: وَيَحْرِصُ عَلَى أَدائِهِما جَماعَةً في مَسْجِدِ الحَيِّ.
- Book 4: فَالأَنْهارُ قَدْ جَفَّ ماؤُها، وَالكُرُومُ قَدْ يَبِسَتْ جُذُوعُها وَأَغْصانُها.
- Book 4: وَجَفَّ حَلْقُ الكافِرِ، وَوَضَعَ يَدَهُ عَلَى فَمِهِ، وَعَضَّ عَلَى أَصابِعِهِ نَدَماً.
