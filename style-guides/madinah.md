# Style guide: Madinah Arabic course books (دروس اللغة العربية لغير الناطقين بها, Dr V. Abdur Raheem)

Write like a patient classroom textbook, not like a novel or a news article. Everything exists to drill ONE grammar point at a time through short, concrete, predictable sentences.

## 1. Voice and register
- Plain Modern Standard Arabic, neutral and polite. No dialect, no slang, no idioms, no figurative language, no humor.
- Teacher-to-student tone. Teacher lines are short questions or commands; students answer with the minimum complete sentence.
- Islamic culture is the default background and is never explained. Use الله، رسول الله ﷺ، رضي الله عنه، عليه السلام، مكة، المسجد، الصلاة، رمضان, and Quranic quotations in the later books as ordinary content.
- Vocabulary is concrete and everyday: school objects, family, house, mosque, market, doctor, car, weather, travel. No abstract or literary words.

## 2. Scenarios and cast
- Cast: the teacher (المدرس / أستاذ / الأستاذ), the مدير (principal), and classmates with Muslim names: حامد، علي، خالد، عباس، هاشم، طلحة، فيصل، زكريا، حمزة، عثمان، جمال، الحسين، الزبير, women and girls: خديجة، آمنة، سعاد. Also الطبيب، التاجر، المهندس، المؤذن، الفلاح.
- Places: الفصل، المكتبة، المدرسة، المسجد، البيت، المطعم، المطار، مكة، المدينة. Students come from many countries (العراق، اليابان، سويسرا، ألمانيا، إنجلترا، السعودية).
- Book 2 and 3 dialogue scenarios: a teacher quizzing students ("who opened the door?", "did you understand the lesson?"); late to class; travel and summer holiday; a restaurant; Hajj/Umrah trips; asking how long someone stayed somewhere.
- Readers (Reading Level 1) use short expository units titled with a place or topic (e.g. مكة المكرمة): 6-8 short sentences giving plain facts, with a "we love / Muslims face the Kaaba" closing line.

## 3. Sentence and paragraph shape by level
- **Book 1 (beginner):** 2-5 words. Nominal sentences only at first: noun + adjective/predicate (الباب مفتوح), إضافة (باب السيارة، ابن المدرس), أسماء الإشارة (هذا / هذه / ذلك / تلك) with a sentence pair "هذا X وذلك Y". Pattern drills: أين + noun? → هو/هي + preposition phrase. من هذا؟ هو ... . أ + هذا ... ؟ نعم / لا. Vocative يا + name (يا محمدُ، يا أستاذُ). Prepositions (في، من، على، تحت، لـ) and nationality "من + country".
- **Book 2 (elementary):** 4-9 words. Introduce the past verb (فتح، كسر، رجع، فهم، كتب) in Q/A pairs: teacher asks "أ + past verb + subject يا + name؟" and the student answers "نعم، فعلتُه" or "لا، ما فعلتُه". Add the negative with ما, object pronouns (ـه، ـها), then كان and its sisters (لا يزال), imperatives and plural/feminine address (اذهبْ، اذهبي، اذهبوا، اذهبن), with Quranic examples.
- **Book 3 (intermediate):** 6-14 words. Present tense with لم, لن, لو, منذ, قط; passive voice (يُسمع، فُتح، قُتل); derived verb forms; اسم الفاعل; full dialogues with short narrated facts; Islamic-history questions (متى ولد النبي ﷺ؟).
- Dialogues use `المدرس : ...` speaker labels, 6-14 turns; exercises use imperative headings (اقرأ واكتب، أجب، املأ الفراغ) and numbering (١) (٢).
- Introduce a pattern with 2-4 example sentences, then recycle it in 10-20 numbered variations that swap only one word.
- Question-answer drills: question then immediate answer on the same line, or a numbered list of comprehension questions (متى؟ كم؟ لماذا؟ أين؟ من الذي؟).
- Fill-in patterns: sentences with a blank line (أين ____ السيارة؟), "كوّن جملاً مفيدة", "صحّح التركيبات", "حوّل الجمل الآتية مثله", with an example (مثال) first.

## 4. Tashkeel
- Book 1: full case endings on most words (الطالبُ مريضٌ), because the case ending is the lesson. Pause forms are used in some exercise lines.
- Books 2-3: fully vowel the new word or the grammar target; leave familiar words bare. Dialogue lines in Book 2 are often fully vowelled on verbs and names.
- Readers: fully vowelled throughout, including shadda and sukun.
- Rule for you: vowel every word in Book 1 and reader-style output; in Book 2-3 style vowel all verbs, names, and the tested grammar point. Never leave case endings wrong.

## 5. DO / DON'T
DO: one grammar point per text; repeat key words; use Muslim names; keep Q/A in pairs; include a fill-in or comprehension exercise at the end; end sentences with a full stop and a space before it as the books do (" .").
DO: use the speaker-label dialogue format.
DON'T: use dialect, idioms, rhetorical flourish, long subordinate chains, rare roots, or grammar above the stated level.
DON'T: add English or transliteration inside the Arabic text.
DON'T: include non-Muslim cultural scenes (dating, alcohol, pork, music); keep content halal and school/family/mosque/market centered.
