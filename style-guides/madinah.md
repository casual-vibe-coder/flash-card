# Style guide: دروس اللغة العربية (Madinah Books)

The Madinah series (دروس اللغة العربية لغير الناطقين بها, Dr V. Abdur-Raheem, Islamic University of Madinah) is a graded drill-book course: Books 1-3 plus the side readers (Reading L1, Expression L1-2). Write like a patient classroom textbook, never like a novel or news article. Every text drills ONE grammar point through short, concrete, predictable sentences. Use the Level section that matches the target level.

## Quick summary
Plain, polite, devout Modern Standard Arabic. Flat, factual, teacher-to-student. Short staccato sentences (Book 1: about 2-6 words; Book 2: about 4-8; Book 3: about 4-9). Almost no narration: texts are picture labels, question-answer pairs and `المدرس : ...` speaker-label dialogues with Muslim male student names. One grammar target recurs 8-20 times, swapping one word per line. Islamic life (prayer times, الحمد لله، إن شاء الله) is ordinary background, never explained or preached. Exercises follow: أجب، املأ الفراغ، حوّل، صحّح. No humour, idioms, dialect or figurative language.

## Voice and register
- Neutral-formal MSA of a classroom. Teacher lines are short questions or gentle commands (اقرأ الدرس يا علي). Students answer with the minimum complete sentence.
- Polite Islamic social register: السلام عليكم ورحمة الله وبركاته to open, الحمد لله، إن شاء الله، ما شاء الله، شفاك الله، زادك الله علماً. ﷺ and رضي الله عنه follow sacred names.
- Concrete everyday vocabulary (school, house, mosque, market, doctor, food, travel). Abstract nouns only: لغة، دين، بلد.
- No moral at the end. Ethics appear as habit (prayer times, du'a for the sick, Qur'an memorisation), not sermon.
- Sentences end with a spaced full stop " ." or " ؟". Exercises are numbered (١) (٢).

## Devices and patterns
- Dialogue format: `المدرس : ...` (label, space, colon, line). No quote marks, no narration. Stage directions only in parentheses on their own line, e.g. (يدخل محمود). Labels are names or roles (المدرس، الأب، الأم، الطلاب). Turns: one short sentence, sometimes 2-3.
- Q-A ladder (signature device): one question repeated across persons, answered with the same verb in a new form (وأين أمك؟ وأين إخوتك؟ ... ذهبت، ذهبوا، ذهبن). Questions are shorter than answers (3-6 words).
- Question words: أ (preferred over هل in Books 1-2), من، ما، ماذا، أين، متى، كم، لماذا، كيف، أيّ.
- Introduce a pattern with 2-4 examples, then recycle it in 10-20 numbered variations changing one slot. Nouns, names and the target form are re-used lesson after lesson.
- Fill-in and transform drills: اقرأ واكتب، أجب عن الأسئلة، املأ الفراغ، ضع في الفراغ حرف جر، حوّل (singular to plural, masculine to feminine), صحّح الجمل، كوّن جملة، رتّب الكلمات، with a مثال first. Each lesson ends with a boxed الكلمات الجديدة list (plural marked ج).
- Connectors: mainly و, then ثم, فـ, لكن, لأنّ (answering لماذا), أم, بعد/قبل. Avoid ولذلك، حيث، رغم أن.
- Cast: المدرس (unnamed), المدير; students حامد، علي، خالد، هاشم، عباس، طلحة، فيصل، زكريا، حمزة، عثمان، الحسين، الزبير، يوسف، إبراهيم; women only as family or roles (زينب، آمنة، خديجة، سعاد، الدكتورة سعاد); also الطبيب، التاجر، المهندس، المؤذن، الفلاح. Students come from many countries.

## Never / avoid
- Never describe faces or eyes (or any facial features) in any image-prompt text; illustrations are simple objects or figures without faces.
- No dialect, slang, idioms, proverbs, metaphor, humour, emoji, exclamation spam (one "ما شاء الله!" at most).
- No English or transliteration inside the Arabic.
- No plot, villains, cliffhangers, romance, politics, brands, modern tech slang; no non-Muslim scenes (dating, alcohol, pork, music).
- No grammar above the stated level; no stacked subordinate clauses; no passive before Book 3; no rare roots.
- No fabricated hadith or Qur'an; quote only real verses (inside ﴿ ﴾) and attribute hadith with source (رواه مسلم).
- Never put wrong case endings; no female-led or mixed-gender scenes.

## Level 1: Book 1 (beginner)
- Sentence length: min 2, typical 3-5 (early 2-3), max about 8; median about 4; one outlier shape is a chained nationality list of 16-17 words.
- Shape: picture labels, Q-A pairs, 10-15 turn dialogues in strict Q-A alternation (2-8 words per turn). True reading passages only in 3 lessons (My House, My School), 55-170 words, 3-5 paragraphs, with a flat closer (أنا أحب أبي وأمي). Pattern: pairs of 2-3 word sentences, then one 6-9 word compound.
- Tashkeel: early lessons and presentation text fully vowelled with case endings; exercise rows bare or only on targets; later dialogues vowel names, odd words and the taught item. Names and foreign places vowelled on first use.
- Scenarios and cast: teacher quizzes student; two classmates introduce themselves (من أنت؟ من أين أنت؟); headmaster asks about the roster; schoolgirls greet their teacher.
- Grammar: هذا/هذه/ذلك/تلك, ما/من هذا؟, أ + نعم/لا, ال, adjective predicate, prepositions (في، على، تحت، أمام، خلف، عند), إضافة, لـ ownership, يا + name, pronouns and suffixes (بيتي، عندي), plurals, past verbs (ذهب، خرج، جلس), dual, كم + تمييز, numbers 3-10, colours, من/إلى + country. No imperfect, future, passive or conditionals.
- Vocabulary: objects, animals, family, professions, school places, countries, languages, colours, cities.
- Samples:
  - هٰذَا بَيْتٌ . / هٰذَا مَسْجِدٌ .
  - أَذلِكَ كَلْبٌ؟ لا، ذلِكَ قِطٌّ .
  - اَلْحِمَارُ صَغِيرٌ وَالْحِصَانُ كَبِيرٌ .
  - هذا بيتي . بيتي أمام المسجد . بيتي جميلٌ .

## Level 2: Book 2
- Sentence length: min 2, typical 4-8 (early 3-6, late 5-11), max about 16-17; median about 5-6; about 75% are 3-9 words.
- Shape: almost 100% dialogue, about 18-30 turns (1-1.5 pages), 1 short sentence per turn. Two lessons start with a 40-60 word narrative paragraph (ثم، فقال) then switch to dialogue. Opens with السلام عليكم (early) or straight with a teacher question (من فتح باب الفصل؟). Ends abruptly or on a wish (شفاك الله، إن شاء الله).
- Tashkeel: selective. Vowel the target form (verb endings, case endings, hamza question) and difficult names; leave function words and common nouns bare. About 15% of words early, 25-35% later; narrative paragraphs 50-60%. Qur'an quotes fully vowelled inside ﴿ ﴾. Write ـاً on adverbs (جيداً، غداً).
- Scenarios and cast: classroom actions (who opened/broke), mother and son, father's daily routine after prayers, absence and lateness, illness, shopping list, holiday and Umrah travel, airport, library, inviting classmates.
- Grammar: إنّ and suffixes, ليس، اسم التفضيل، full past paradigm with ما negation, ما أجمله!, الذي/التي, present tense, imperatives, لا الناهية, أن + منصوب، لن، لم، لأنّ، لـ + مضارع، منذ، dual, sound masculine plural, hollow and defective verbs.
- Vocabulary: nationality and family, classroom, home, university, prayer times, medicine, travel, market goods.
- Samples:
  - مَنْ فَتَحَ بابَ الفَصْلِ؟ - أنا فَتَحْتُهُ.
  - مَنْ خَلَقَكَ يا إبراهيم؟ - خَلَقَنِي اللَّهُ.
  - إنِّي لَنْ أذهبَ إلى الهند في عطلة الصيف.
  - شَفَاكَ اللهُ شفاءً كاملاً.

## Level 3: Book 3 and the side readers (reading level 1, expression levels 1-2)
- Sentence length: Book 3 dialogue turns min 2, typical 4-7, max 9-10; rule examples 3-6; Reading L1 turns min 1-2, typical 3-6, max 8; Expression L1 prompts 3-8 (max 9); Expression L2 typical 5-10, up to 12-14.
- Shape: no stories. Book 3 dialogues 15-25 turns, closing with the teacher asking a student to recite an ayah. Reading L1 dialogues 8-14 turns, every one opens with the full salam greeting and titled by topic (مطعم الجامعة). Expression books are instruction-voice exercises addressed to "you" (أجب، رتّب الكلمات، تخيل أنك...).
- Tashkeel: Reading L1 fully vowelled including shadda. Book 3 vowels the target in each line (30-70%); Qur'an always full. Expression L1 medium, Expression L2 sparse (rare or ambiguous words only).
- Scenarios and cast: male students of the Islamic University (حامد، خالد، عارف، الحسن، إسحاق) and المدرس or المراقب; exam results, broken lock, asking permission to go to hospital, sports club, cafeteria, grocer, airport, letter to father, post office, Ramadan and Eid, zoo.
- Grammar: Book 3 adds passive, اسم الفاعل and derived verbs, مقصور/منقوص/ممدود, واو الحال, شرط (إن، إذا، لو), لام الأمر, لم/لن/قد/سوف, قطّ, كم الخبرية. Readers use simple past and present, هيا بنا، عندما، ثم، لأن.
- Vocabulary: campus, mosque, prayer, food, health and pharmacy, money and prices (بكم القلم؟), travel, letters, Ramadan, grammar terms.
- Samples:
  - هَيَّا بِنَا إِلَى شعبة تعليم اللغة العربية.
  - مَاذَا فَعَلَ يَاسِرٌ أَمْسِ يَا قَاسِمُ؟
  - أَدْرُسُ اللُّغَةَ العَرَبِيَّةَ لأَفْهَمَ لُغَةَ القُرْآنِ الكَرِيمِ.
  - هل مِنْ ذاهبٍ إلى السوق اليوم؟
