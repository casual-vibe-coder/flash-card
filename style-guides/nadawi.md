# Style guide: أبو الحسن علي الندوي (Al-Nadawi)

Corpus: the graded reader القراءة الراشدة (3 parts) and the one-volume قصص النبيين (Ibrahim to Isa). Pick the Level section for the learner, or the Prophet stories section for any story. Counts are hand counts (+/-2 words). [WEB] marks secondary material.

## Quick summary

A grandfather-teacher who thinks in Qur'anic cadence. Short, fully vowelled lines stacked one per row, each opening with وَ or فَ. The name is repeated, never a pronoun. One frame is hammered four to six times with a single word swapped, then a sharp وَلَكِنَّ turns the plot. God is the quiet agent (وَأَرَادَ اللهُ أَنْ ...). Idols are exposed as stone by child-logic; tyrants are pitied and mocked (مِسْكِينٌ فِرْعَوْنُ!). Dialogue is a colon and one line per speaker, no quotation marks; the verse itself is the character's speech, then glossed plainly. Lessons end without a lecture: a du'a, an ayah, a date of death, a vow, or a two-word punch (اللهُ!). Length climbs with the learner: median 6 words a line in early stories, 8 in Part 2, 17 in Part 3, 30-35 in late prophet chapters. Devout, warm, never dialect.

## Voice and register

- Fusha in simple classical syntax, never colloquial, even in children's chat or crowd thoughts. The one near-colloquial exclamation in 633 pages is يَا سَلامُ!
- A kind, earnest teacher. No jokes or irony about faith; the only humour is contempt for idols and tyrants ("وَكَانَ الْمَلِكُ بَلِيداً جِدّاً، وَكَذَلِكَ كُلُّ مُشْرِكٍ").
- Narrator by level: first-person child "أنا" (Part 1); third-person storyteller or historian (Part 2, prophet stories); first-person adult dreamer or a speaking object (Part 3: "أنا تراب"، "المنارة تتحدث"). Father-and-son dialogue is a staple.
- Prophet stories use an impersonal narrator, no "I" and no "my child" (two exceptions in 288 pages). Later chapters address a plural audience: "قَرَأْتُمْ"، "اقْرَؤُوا".
- Emotion rides on a short verb pair, an exclamation or a question: "تَعَجَّبَ النَّاسُ وَدَهِشُوا"، "سَكَتَ النَّاسُ وَخَجِلُوا". Characters get adjective pairs ("عَاقِلاً حَلِيماً")، real Muslim names and honorifics (ﷺ، عليه السلام، رضي الله عنه).

## Devices and patterns

- **Openers.** About 85% of story chapters open with وَ/فَ + verb, as if one oral telling were cut into scenes: وَكَانَ + name + imperfect; وَلَمَّا ...; وَأَرَادَ اللهُ أَنْ ...; "ذَاتَ لَيْلَةٍ". Reader lessons open on a motion verb, a habit, a question to the child, or a speaker label. Biographies: "وُلِدَ ... سَنَةَ (...)".
- **Transitions.** ثُمَّ stage; فَ result; وَلَكِنَّ the turn; هُنَالِكَ/وَهُنَا the pivot; وَهَكَذَا the outcome; فَإِذَا بِ surprise; أَمَّا ... فَ contrast.
- **Closers.** By frequency: an ayah on its own line; a result line; an exclamation; a suspense question handing to the next chapter; a du'a or salawat; a death date. Never a labelled moral.
- **Parataxis.** Clauses chain with و/ف/ثم and spaced commas; subordination is rare (لَمَّا، حَتَّى، لِأَنَّ). Hal clauses with وَهُوَ/وَقَدْ.
- **Triads and doublets.** "فَأَكَلُوا وَشَبِعُوا وَشَكَرُوا"; paired synonyms; four-fold negation ("لا يَشْكُو وَلا يَتَعَتَّبُ وَلا يَتَذَمَّرُ وَلا يَغْضَبُ").
- **Rhetorical questions.** Bursts of 2-5 (أَلا/هَلْ), closed with "؟!". About 1 sentence in 3 ends in ! or ؟ in the Musa chapters; none in the Isa chapters.
- **Similes.** One concrete image from home or nature (كَوُحُوشِ الْغَابَةِ، كَالأَطْفَالِ الصِّغَارِ); none at Level 1.
- **Vocatives and saj'.** Vocatives only in speech (يَا أَبِي، يَا قَوْمِ، يَا وَلَدِي). Saj' is light, 2-3 clause bursts (دَوْلَةٌ وَصَوْلَةٌ، مِحْنَةٌ وَمِنْحَةٌ); full rhyme only in poems and du'a.
- **Repetition frames.** A ladder of 4-6 lines (وَكَانَ يُوسُفُ يَعْلَمُ أَنَّ ...); a refrain 5-12 times (وَأَنَا هُنَا، فَاعْرِفُونِي); a chain where each end seeds the next (لَمْ يَصِلْ إِلَى الْقَمَرِ فَضْلاً عَنِ الشَّمْسِ ...); doubled words ("أَبَداً؟ أَبَداً؟").
- **Quoted speech.** قَالَ + colon, one turn per line, no quotation marks; a manner phrase may precede ("فَقَالَ فِي وَقَارٍ وَشَفَقَةٍ:"). « » only for hadith, du'a, non-Qur'anic scripture; ﴿ ﴾ for Qur'an.
- **Qur'an.** In children's chapters never "قال الله تعالى", no surah names. The character's verse follows a bare قَالَ, or the narrator paraphrases and the ayah seals it. About 45% of lines on many Musa and Dawud pages are ayat. In readers an ayah is a closing seal or a whole-surah lesson (Hujurat, Saff).
- **Hadith and poetry.** Hadith retold as story, isnad cut to the companion, sayings in « » fully vowelled, source in brackets. A whole poem is its own lesson, poet in brackets; in prose, one line after "وَفِي ذَلِكَ يَقُولُ الشَّاعِرُ:".

## Signature phrasing

- وَكَانَ + اسْمٌ + مُضَارِعٌ: setting frame (وَكَانَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ يَعْرِفُ ...).
- وَلَكِنَّ + الاسم: the pivot (وَلَكِنَّ يُوسُفَ لَمْ يَسْتَعْجِلْ).
- وَلَمَّا ... فَ: time clause, then result.
- هُنَالِكَ / وَهُنَا: turning point (هُنَالِكَ غَضِبَ مُوسَى).
- وَهَكَذَا: outcome hinge (وَهَكَذَا أَخَذَ مُحَمَّدٌ الْقُسْطَنْطِينِيَّةَ).
- فَإِذَا بِـ: sudden event (فَإِذَا فِي الدَّلْوِ غُلامٌ!).
- وَأَرَادَ اللهُ أَنْ ...: divine will opens a turn.
- ثُمَّ مَاذَا؟: drives a dialogue lesson.
- أَمَّا ... فَ: contrast (أَمَّا الْجُلُوسُ فَنَعَمْ! وَأَمَّا الْعِشَاءُ فَلا).
- لَمْ ... وَلَمْ ...: twin negation (لَمْ يَعْجِزْ وَلَمْ يَيْأَسْ).
- لا ... وَلا ...: idol litany (لا تَضُرُّ وَلا تَنْفَعُ).
- جِدّاً / كَثِيراً: final intensifier (كَثِيرَةٌ جِدّاً).
- مَا أَشَدَّ الْحَرَّ! يَا لَطِيفُ!: wonder exclamation.
- انْظُرُوا إِلَى ... كَيْفَ خُلِقَتْ: guides the eye.
- هَلْ سَمِعْتَ بِـ ...؟: hook question.
- حُبّاً وَكَرَامَةً: polite acceptance.
- تَفَضَّلْ / عَلَى اسْمِ اللهِ / إِنْ شَاءَ اللهُ: stock courtesies.
- فَأَجَابَ اللهُ دَعْوَتَهُ وَأَعَانَهُ: answered-du'a closer.
- وَلِلَّهِ الأَمْرُ مِنْ قَبْلُ وَمِنْ بَعْدُ: closing seal.
- مِنْ عَجَائِبِ صُنْعِ اللهِ: nature-lesson tag.
- وَكَذَلِكَ كُلُّ مَنْ لا يَخَافُ اللهَ: generalising tag.
- مِسْكِينٌ فِرْعَوْنُ!: mock pity.
- وَقَدْ + مَاضٍ: background state (وَقَدْ جَلَسَ عَلَى سَرِيرٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ).
- لَوْ كَانَ أَحَدٌ مَكَانَ يُوسُفَ ...: teaching counterfactual.
- رَأَيْتُ ... وَرَأَيْتُ ...: vision anaphora (Level 3).
- مَا لَبِثْتُ أَنْ ...: narrator's reaction beat (Level 3).
- وَقَدْ آلَيْتُ عَلَى نَفْسِي أَنْ ...: vow closer (Level 3).
- شَدِيدَ الذَّكَاءِ، سَدِيدَ النَّظَرِ: portrait chain (Level 3).
- رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ / رَحِمَهُ اللهُ: after pious names.

## Never / avoid

- Never describe faces or eyes in image-prompt text. Write prompts as action, hands, backs, distance and objects only. The source never portrays a prophet's or companion's appearance ("جَمِيلٌ" and "لاغِباً" are the only looks given); "عُيُونُ الْغُرَابِ" and "تَدْمَعُ الْعُيُونُ" are figures of speech.
- No dialect, slang, English or transliteration; rare words get a footnote or bracketed gloss.
- No irony about faith, God or prophets; mockery is for idols, tyrants and obstinate elites only.
- No quotation marks for ordinary speech; no bullets, headings or numbered lists inside a lesson.
- No labelled moral ("العبرة"، "الدرس"); the moral is an event, a closing line, a hadith or a one-line aphorism.
- Do not invent Qur'anic text, miracles, names (the whale, the ant) or dialogue for prophets beyond the Qur'an or hadith; invention is limited to domestic glue and stated motives.
- No speeches over 5-6 lines (except the Qur'an) and no dialogue over 4 turns.
- Early levels: no subordinate chains, abstract jargon, pronoun chains or digits in prose.

## Level 1: القراءة الراشدة part 1

- Sentences (about 134 counted, lessons 1-31): min 1, median 7-8, typical max 25. Dialogue turns median 5 (3-11). A routine or travel lesson may open with one comma chain of 55-75 words (about 110 in the train lesson).
- Shape: 31 lessons, pp14-88; 150-450 words, 1-3 pages, 3-8 paragraphs of 2-6 sentences; ends "* * *". Of 15 lessons seen: 5 first-person narratives, 3 hadith or companion stories, 3 pious tales, 1 dialogue play, 2 descriptions, 2 poems.
- Tashkeel: about 100%, with case endings, tanwin, shadda and vowelled speaker labels; titles carry kashida. Dialogue pages slightly lighter.
- Themes: a boy's day and prayers, bargaining, picnic, train, "what will you be?", race, first fast, hunting, feast, parents, dignity of work, rain. Prayer times mark the clock.
- Register: concrete home, school and market nouns, recycled on purpose; present routines and كان + imperfect; doublets (بِأَدَبٍ وَسَكِينَةٍ). Hadith and du'a appear, ayat do not.
- Samples:
  - أَنَامُ مُبَكِّراً فِي اللَّيْلِ وَأَقُومُ مُبَكِّراً فِي الصَّبَاحِ [r1 p14]
  - تِلْكَ عَادَتِي كُلَّ يَوْمٍ لاَ أُخَالِفُهَا [r1 p15]
  - فَأَكَلْنَا وَشَبِعْنَا وَنِمْنَا قَلِيلاً [r2 p43]
  - فَالأَرْضُ خَضْرَاءُ وَالْحُقُولُ خَضْرَاءُ وَالدُّنْيَا كُلُّهَا خَضْرَاءُ [r2 p60]
  - قَالَ الْمُشْرِكُ: مَنْ يَمْنَعُكَ مِنِّي؟ قَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ: اللهُ! [r1 p28]
  - وَمَا كَانَ يَوْمٌ أَجْمَلَ فِي حَيَاتِي مِنْ ذَلِكَ الْيَوْمِ [r2 p44]

## Level 2: القراءة الراشدة part 2 and sira vignettes

- Sentences (about 169 counted, lessons 32-73): slice medians 6-9, pooled 8, min 1, max 45 (two evocative lists reach 55-60). Dialogue median 4-5. Every described feature is paired with its use ("وَرَأْسُهُ صَغِيرٌ لِيَكُونَ خَفِيفَ الْحَمْلِ").
- Shape: 42 lessons, pp90-192; 1-3 pages (up to 5); paragraphs of 3-7 sentences (40-90 words). Biographies open "وُلِدَ ... سَنَةَ" and end on a death date, a du'a or a quoted saying. Science comes as father-son Q&A or as a speaking object with a refrain.
- Tashkeel: about 85-100% in narrative; about 50% (key words and endings) on some dialogue pages. Ayat, poems, du'a full.
- Themes: boy-Companions (Umayr, Rafi', Samura), the Black Stone, orphans, Eid, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, Imam Malik, al-Fatih, cleanliness, the eye, lion, camel, locomotive, plants, steamship, moon; poems by Abu al-Atahiya.
- Samples:
  - فَكَّرَ مُحَمَّدٌ، وَلَمْ يَعْجِزْ وَلَمْ يَيْأَسْ، وَوَجَدَ حِيلَةً! [r5 p149]
  - انْظُرُوا إِلَى الْإِبِلِ: كَيْفَ خُلِقَتْ [r5 p141]
  - لَا تَسْأَلُوا عَنْ فَرَحِ عُمَيْرٍ وَسُرُورِهِ [r4 p107]
  - الْأَسَدُ مَلِكُ الْغَابَةِ ، وَسَيِّدُ السِّبَاعِ [r4 p128]
  - لَقَدْ ثَقُلَ عَمَلُ يَوْمٍ وَاحِدٍ فَكَيْفَ إِذَا اجْتَمَعَ عَمَلُ يَوْمَيْنِ؟ [r5 p155]
  - أَنَا تُرَابٌ حَقِيرٌ يَطَؤُنِي النَّاسُ بِأَقْدَامِهِمْ [r5 p175]

## Level 3: القراءة الراشدة part 3 and the long essays

- Sentences: history and dream-vision prose (n=40) min 4, median 17, max 75. Anecdote with dialogue (n=41) min 2, median 10, max about 90 (one virtue list); replies 2-8 words. Evocative lists reach 40-60. Exercise questions (n=87) median 5-6, max 30.
- Shape: 20 lessons, pp196-275. Serials run 4.5-6.3 pages (من النجوم إلى الأرض، المنارة تتحدث x3); anecdotes 2-3; biographies 4.5-5.5. Open "خَرَجْتُ يَوْماً ..." or a birth date. Close on a vow, a wake-up frame, an ayah or an epithet chain; a bracketed source follows ([البداية والنهاية ج٧ ص٤٠]).
- Tashkeel: 55-70% of content words in narrative, 45-60% in biography and memoir; every case ending in lists and idafa chains; poems and ayat 100%. Whole-surah lessons carry no commentary.
- Themes: Andalus and Delhi history, Ghazali, Ibn Taymiyya, Farrukh and Malik, Rib'i before Rustam, Umar and the widow, a secret Muslim childhood under the Inquisition, five poems. The narrator weeps three times in seven pages.
- Register: formal literary MSA, historical terms (الجزية، النمارق), Persian and Indian names, dates in brackets (٦٦١), antithesis (رُهْبَانٌ بِاللَّيْلِ فُرْسَانٌ بِالنَّهَارِ), oaths, light سجع.
- Apparatus: 5 numbered questions (recall, then why or how, then opinion "وَلِمَاذَا؟") plus one grammar drill under ❊; a glossary of about 230 entries as "الكلمة: شرح".
- Samples:
  - خَرَجْتُ يَوماً مِنْ مَدِينَةِ دِهْلِي أُرَوِّحُ نَفْسِي مِنْ صَخَبِ الأَسْوَاقِ [r7 p218]
  - لم أَرَ كاليومِ حِجارةً تَنْطِقُ، ومنارةً تتحدَّثُ! [r7 p219]
  - وقمتُ وقد آلَيْتُ على نفسي أن أكونَ جُنْدِيّاً للإسلام [r7 p217]
  - كان الغزاليُّ شديدَ الذكاء، سديدَ النَّظَر، عجيبَ الفِطْرة [r7 p245]
  - فواللهِ ما ضَيَّعْتُه [r8 p248]
  - وكم عذَّبوا! وكم نُفُوا من بلادهم، وضَيَّعوا من مسلم! [r8 p265]

## Prophet stories (قصص النبيين) mode

- Structure: numbered boxed chapters with 2-4 word noun titles (بَعْدَ يُوسُفَ). Counts from the index: Ibrahim 16, Yusuf 25, Nuh 22, Hud 11, Salih 13, Musa 26+, Shu'ayb 11, Dawud and Sulayman 15, Ayyub and Yunus 7, Zakariyya 8, Isa 42. A chapter is 3 lines to 1.5 pages (30-200 words). Book opens "قَبْلَ أَيَّامٍ كَثِيرَةٍ. كَثِيرَةٍ جِدًّا."
- Line length by stage (one sentence per line):
  - A, Ibrahim to Nuh (pp9-72, n=100): median 6, min 2, max 17.
  - B, Nuh to Salih and Musa's birth (pp73-144, n=121): median 7-8, max 24; 80% run 4-10 words.
  - C, Musa to the Red Sea (pp145-214, n=114): median 8-10, max 19 (one 95-word recap).
  - D, Shu'ayb to Zakariyya (pp215-249, n=50): median 29-30, max 120; hal chains, epithet lists.
  - E, Isa (pp250-279, n=45): median 35, max 88; historian's voice, no questions. Advanced learners only.
- Repetition: a 4-6 line ladder with one slot changed; an idol litany (لا تَنْفَعُ ... لا تَضُرُّ ...); a name refrain 4-5 times (وَلَكِنَّ الطِّفْلَ يَبْكِي وَيَأْبَى); mirrored contrast (الْكَوْكَبُ ضَعِيفٌ ... الْقَمَرُ ضَعِيفٌ ...); 2-4 parallel lines before the turn.
- Dialogue: prophet against father, king, elite or crowd, a line per speaker, "يَا قَوْمِ". The prophet reasons gently; the elite taunts ("مِنْ مَتَى صِرْتَ نَجَّاراً؟"). An ayah sits on its own line, then a plain restatement.
- Miracles and tests: told as the Qur'an gives them, no extra detail. Seven beats: blessings, ingratitude and idols, gentle da'wa, the elite mocks, the sign (ark, she-camel, staff), catastrophe in short clauses ("تَقْلَعُ الأَشْجَارَ، وَتَهْدِمُ الْبُيُوتَ"), believers saved plus a verdict ayah. Tests meet a named virtue: لَمْ يَسْتَعْجِلْ، لَمْ يَجْزَعْ، لَمْ يَيْأَسْ.
- Endings: an ayah; a result line (وَخَرَجَ يُوسُفُ بَرِيئاً وَأَكْرَمَهُ الْمَلِكُ); an exclamation; a cliffhanger question; "صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَى إِبْرَاهِيمَ الْخَلِيلِ وَسَلَّمَ". A story ends in an ornament trio (❁).
- Moral: one aphorism with إِنَّ (إِنَّ الأَنْبِيَاءَ يَجُودُونَ بِالْخَيْرِ فِي كُلِّ زَمَانٍ), a counterfactual, a jab at the tyrant, or a tie to the child's life (هَلْ شَرِبْتَ مَاءَ زَمْزَمَ؟).
- Tashkeel: full with case endings, about 95-100%; ayat in Uthmani full; footnotes bare.
- Samples:
  - وَكَانَ آزَرُ يَبِيعُ الأَصْنَامَ. [s1 p9]
  - وَكَانَ يَعْرِفُ أَنَّ الأَصْنَامَ لا تَضُرُّ وَلا تَنْفَعُ. [s1 p10]
  - وَلَكِنَّ يُوسُفَ لَمْ يَسْتَعْجِلْ. [s2 p39]
  - أَمْطَرَتِ السَّمَاءُ وَأَمْطَرَتْ وَأَمْطَرَتْ [s3 p79]
  - وَقَامَ فِرْعَوْنُ وَقَعَدَ وَبَرَقَ فِرْعَوْنُ وَرَعَدَ. [s5 p155]
  - وَلَكِنَّ الطِّفْلَ يَبْكِي وَيَأْبَى. [s4 p124]

## Beyond the readers

[WEB, secondary] Only a few raw pages were opened; these are hints, not measurements.

- ماذا خسر العالم بانحطاط المسلمين (adult argument): the first sentence runs about 149 words with 20 commas; the first paragraph has 2 sentences (mean about 47, median 13). It uses periodic antithesis, "لا ... بل", a closing ayah, then slogans of about 6 words.
- قصص النبيين للأطفال Part 1 (1942) confirms the youngest register: 3-7 word lines and the refrain "وَعَرَفَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ أَنَّ اللهَ رَبُّهُ". Part 5 (the sirah volume, not in the owned PDF) reads as flowing clauses with verb tricolons ("..., فَمَزَّقَهُ، وَقَالَ ...") and scene headings ("غَطْرَسَةُ كِسْرَى وَعِقَابُهَا").
- السيرة النبوية، الطريق إلى المدينة، مذكرات سائح: only descriptions seen; make no style claim.
- Late chapters of the owned PDF (median 30-35) already resemble his adult prose, so Level 3 plus Stages D-E suit advanced learners.
