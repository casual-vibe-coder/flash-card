# Style guide: أبو الحسن علي الندوي (Al-Nadawi)

Corpus: the graded school reader القراءة الراشدة (parts 1-3, with comprehension drills and a glossary) and the children's book قصص النبيين. Use this guide to write Arabic that reads like these books. Pick the Level section that matches the learner, or the Prophet stories section when the content is a story.

## Quick summary

Warm, devout, plain fusha from a gentle teacher. Every text carries a moral or faith frame, and God is the agent behind events. Sentences are paratactic chains joined by و and ف, with direct dialogue in the form "قال X: ..." and no quotation marks. Short, concrete, repetitive lines at the low levels (4-10 words, one idea per line, the name repeated rather than a pronoun). Long comma-chained periods and balanced pairs at the top (25-45 words). Case endings and shadda are marked. Quranic and prophetic echoes appear throughout. Close with praise, a du'a, a verse or a short moral, never a lecture. No irony, no slang, no dialect.

## Voice and register

- Fusha throughout, with simple classical syntax in the Quranic cadence. The narrator is a kind, earnest teacher. There is no irony and no jokes, only mild boyish pride or mild contempt for idols and tyrants.
- Narrator: first-person child in reader part 1; third-person storyteller or historian elsewhere. Personified voices are possible ("أنا تراب", "المنارة تتحدث"). Father-and-son teaching dialogue is a staple.
- Emotion is carried by exclamations and rhetorical questions, not adjectives: "الله أكبر! كيف ...؟", "سبحان الله!", "ما أشد الحر!".
- Characters have real Muslim names and stock epithets (عاقل حليم، ملك جبار متكبر). Honorifics follow names: ﷺ، عليه السلام، رضي الله عنه، رحمه الله.
- Modernity appears only as a sign of God's power (trains, steamships, post). No phones, shops of brands, romance or fantasy.

## Devices and patterns

- Openers: a time or place frame ("ذات ليلة"، "وجاء يوم عيد"، "خرجت يوماً من ..."), "وكان + noun + present verb", "ولما ...", "ثم ...", or a speaker label. About 80% of story sentences begin with و or ف.
- Transitions: ثم، فلما، حتى إذا، وبينما، وهنا، حينئذ، وإذا بـ (surprise), ولكن (the engine of conflict), بل (correction), لأن (cause).
- Repetition is the signature: one frame repeated with one slot changed ("لا تضر ولا تنفع / لا تتكلم ولا تسمع"); the name repeated every line; doubled words ("أبدا؟ أبدا؟"، "كثيرة جدا"); refrains ("وأنا هنا، فاعرفوني!"); anaphora ("أتظنون أنكم ...؟" x4); counted retries ("وثالثة ورابعة وخامسة").
- Dialogue: speaker verb, colon, speech, one turn per line, no quotation marks. Replies are short, then a polite close ("شكراً لك"، "حباً وكرامة"). « » and ﴿ ﴾ are used only for hadith, Quran, du'a and names.
- Quran: quote a verse, then paraphrase it plainly in the next line. Quote only verses you are sure of, with full tashkeel.
- Closers: praise or du'a ("فحمدت الله"), a verse or hadith, or a plot-result line. Units end with "* * *". Titles are numbered and short, with plain nouns ("بائع الأصنام").
- Moral framing is implicit in events: tawhid, patience, trust in God, honest work, gratitude, kindness to parents, the fall of falsehood.

## Never / avoid

- Never describe faces or eyes in any image-prompt text. Keep prophets and companions to action and speech only. Never depict the prophets' appearance.
- No dialect, slang, English or transliteration; no irony or sarcasm; no suspense gimmicks or modern psychology.
- No quotation marks for ordinary dialogue; no bullet lists or headings inside lessons.
- Do not invent Quranic text, miracles or dialogue with God, or unattested details.
- No graphic violence: punishment is told in nature terms and one line.
- No long dialogue (more than 3-4 turns) and no speeches over 5-6 lines apart from the Quran.
- No lesson without a moral or religious frame; never end on pure description.
- At lower levels, no long subordinate chains and no abstract jargon.

## Level 1: القراءة الراشدة part 1 (simple first-person routines and short lessons)

- Sentences: min 4, typical 6-12, max about 40. The first paragraph of a lesson is sometimes one long و/ثم chain. Dialogue turns run 2 to 17 words, typically 4-9.
- Shape: 3-8 paragraphs of 2-5 lines (about 25-60 words), 150-500 words per lesson, ending with "* * *".
- Tashkeel: full or near-full (about 90-100%), with case endings, tanween and shadda. Poems are fully voweled.
- Themes: a Muslim boy's day and prayers, market and bargaining, a picnic, a train journey, "what will you be?", a race, fasting, Eid, the post office, adab of eating, the dignity of work.
- Register: concrete household and school nouns, with repeated vocabulary. Present-tense routines and kān + imperfect. Cast: father, mother, friends خالد وعمر، teacher.
- Samples:
  - أَنَامُ مُبَكِّراً في اللَّيْلِ وَأَقُومُ مُبَكِّراً في الصَّبَاحِ
  - وَكَانَ الطَّعَامُ شَهِيّاً لَذِيذاً
  - أَنَا أُحِبُّ أَنْ أَكُونَ جُنْدِيّاً قَوِيّاً
  - تِلْكَ عَادَتِي كُلَّ يَوْمٍ لَا أُخَالِفُهَا

## Level 2: القراءة الراشدة part 2 and sira vignettes

- Sentences: min 3, typical 8-16, max about 45 (descriptive lessons such as the lion and the shirt). Expository dialogue turns run 10-25 words.
- Shape: third person, past narrative, with a biography opening ("ولد ... سنة ..."). Paragraphs of 3-8 lines (50-110 words), 1-3 pages per lesson. A moral or praise line closes it.
- Tashkeel: about 50-80% of words (lighter, about 40-60%, on some pages). Verb vowels, tanween, shadda and hamza are marked; case endings are inconsistent. Poems and hadith are full.
- Themes: young Companions at Uhud (Rafi' and Samura), orphans, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, Imam Malik, Muhammad al-Fatih, cleanliness, heat, the lion, the locomotive and plants taught by a father to his son.
- Register: passive, relative clauses, لو/إذا conditionals, comparatives, similes with كـ, exclamations. Hijri dates appear.
- Samples:
  - فَفَرِحَ رَافِعٌ كَثِيراً لَمَّا أَذِنَ رَسُولُ اللهِ
  - مَا أَشَدَّ الْحَرَّ! يَا لَطِيفُ!
  - الأَسَدُ مَلِكُ الْغَابَةِ، وَسَيِّدُ السِّبَاعِ
  - انْظُرْ يَا رَشِيدُ! إِلَى هَذَا الْمَوْقِدِ

## Level 3: القراءة الراشدة part 3 and the long classical-style essays

- Sentences: dialogue 2-28 words (typically 8-20). Essay sentences min 8, typical 20-35, max about 60. Biography and history run 25-45, max about 65, inflated by dates, names and appositives.
- Shape: 6-12 paragraphs of 3-9 lines (about 50-120 words), 3-6 pages per lesson. Bracketed source citations follow anecdotes. A hadith or verse closes it.
- Tashkeel: about 60-80% of words, lower (about 50%) in history and higher (about 70%) in biography. Case endings and shadda are marked; common particles and names are bare. Quran and poems are fully voweled.
- Themes: Medina in the Prophet's time, history seen from the stars, a minaret narrating India's past, Ghazali, Ibn Taymiyya, the envoy before Rustam, Andalus, scholars and their mothers.
- Register: abstract and moral terms (ورع، زهد، عدل). Idafa chains, adjective chains (شديد الذكاء، عالي الهمة), anaphora ("ورأيتُ ... ورأيتُ ..."), triads, oaths, and light سجع, never forced.
- Samples:
  - وَمِنْ بَطْنِي يَخْرُجُ الذَّهَبُ وَالْفِضَّةُ، وَالنُّحَاسُ وَالْحَدِيدُ
  - خَرَجْتُ يَوْماً مِنْ مَدِينَةِ دِهْلِي أُرَوِّحُ نَفْسِي
  - وَكَانَ مُحَافِظاً عَلَى الصَّلاةِ وَالصَّوْمِ، مُعَظِّماً لِلشَّرَائِعِ
  - صَادِقِي الْوَعْدِ، سَدِيدِي الْقَوْلِ فِي الْمَسَاجِدِ وَالأَسْوَاقِ

## Prophet stories (قصص النبيين) mode

- Structure: a story is a chain of 15-25 numbered chapters of 1-1.5 pages, each with a plain noun title ("إبراهيم يكسر الأصنام"). Each chapter opens with وكان / وجاء / ولما / a time phrase. The story is always third person, with no narrator "I" and no address to the child. Begin with "قبل أيام كثيرة. كثيرة جدا".
- Line length: early chapters have 3-9 words (typical 4-7), one sentence per line. Middle chapters run 5-9 and late chapters 5-10 (max 15). Paragraphs grow from one line to 2-4 sentences (20-45 words). Dialogue lines run 3-8 words and alternate. Later chapters (Musa and the prophets after him) lengthen to 8-14 words and, in the essay-like Isa chapters, to 12-18 or more. Keep those for advanced learners only.
- Repetition: reuse one frame 4-6 times with a single slot changed. Repeat the name rather than using a pronoun. Place 2-4 parallel lines before the turn of the plot. Contrast with ولكن. Use mirrored contrast ("الكوكب ضعيف ... القمر ضعيف ...").
- Dialogue: debate between a prophet and an idol, father, king or people ("يا قوم ..."), as short exchanges with قال X: on each line. Quote a verse in ﴿ ﴾ and paraphrase it directly.
- Endings: a plot result ("وخرج يوسف بريئا وأكرمه الملك"), a short moral, a verse, or a salawat line. No lecture. The wording is tied to the Quran; invent nothing.
- Tashkeel: full including i'rab, about 85-100%. Quranic verses are always full. Numbers are written as words.
- Samples:
  - وَكَانَ آزَرُ يَبِيعُ الأَصْنَامَ.
  - وَكَانَ يَعْرِفُ أَنَّ الأَصْنَامَ لا تَضُرُّ وَلا تَنْفَعُ.
  - قَالَ الْمَلِكُ: مَنْ رَبُّكَ يَا إِبْرَاهِيمُ؟
  - وَلَكِنَّ الطِّفْلَ يَبْكِي وَيَأْبَى.
