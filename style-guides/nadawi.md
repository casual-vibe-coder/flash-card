# Style guide: Abul Hasan Ali al-Nadawi (readers and prophet stories)

Write Arabic that sounds like a learner reader by al-Nadawi: clear, devout, concrete, morally purposeful. Scope: *Qasas al-Nabiyyin* (children's prophet stories) and *al-Qira'a al-Rashida* parts 1-3.

## 1. Voice & register
- Fusha throughout, with no dialect and no slang. The tone is that of a gentle teacher or storyteller speaking to a child or a beginner.
- Moral and faith framing is the point of every text. Events show Allah's power and care, trust in Allah (tawakkul), and the fall of falsehood and idols.
- Echo the Quran and the Sunna naturally. Use short ayat in ornate brackets, and phrases such as "على الله", "في سبيل الله" and "رضي الله عن ...". Pray for the sahaba after naming them (e.g. "رضي الله عن رافع وسمرة، ورزقنا اتباعهما").
- Use exclamation and rhetorical questions for wonder, in the prophet stories only (e.g. "الله أكبر! كيف تضع الأم ...؟!").
- Do not make jokes or use irony. Keep the narrator warm but serious.

## 2. Content and structure
- **Prophet stories:** a numbered title that names the event ("إبراهيم يكسر الأصنام"). Begin directly with action and a time marker ("وجاء يوم عيد"). Then give a quick sequence of events with short dialogue (قال ... فقال ...). Show the climax through concrete acts such as taking the axe, breaking the idols or hanging the axe on the big idol's neck. End on the result or an open image, not a lecture. Stories continue across chapters.
- **Reader, part 1:** everyday first-person routines and places: daily schedule, school, family, the village river, Friday and holiday habits. These close with a summary sentence ("تلك عادتي كل يوم لا أخالفها").
- **Reader, parts 2-3:** short Islamic history and sira vignettes (children and companions at Uhud and Badr, the envoy and Rustam), then reflective essays on the umma, the early Muslims and their character. Prefer concrete scenes with named people and speech.
- Close a unit with a divider (* * *). Reader units end with a vocabulary list (word: simple gloss) and comprehension questions (فراغات and أسئلة) in plain voweled-or-unvoweled Arabic.
- Typical transitions are "ثم", "وكان", "فلما", "حتى إذا", "حينئذ" and "وبينما".

## 3. Sentence and paragraph shape
- Parataxis dominates: chains of short clauses joined by "و" and "ف". Prophet stories use a single clause per line, 4-10 words, one idea each, often starting "و + verb + subject".
- Repeat words and structures deliberately for rhythm and memorisation ("وخرج الناس ... وخرج الأطفال"; "وسكتت الأصنام ... وسكتت الأصنام وما نطقت").
- Part 1: 6-12 words per sentence, present tense, first person, common nouns, and very few idafa chains.
- Part 2: 12-25 words, past-tense narrative, reported speech, and small subordination with "لما", "لو", "أن", "لأن".
- Part 3: 25-60 word periods, balanced pairs and triads ("أحكام الاجتماع في الاجتماع، وأحكام التجارة في التجارة ..."; "في المسجد ... وفي الصلاة ..."), a similes ("شأن الذي يتعلم السباحة في بحر متلاطم"), and rhymed or rhythmic clause endings (a light سجع, never forced). Use Quranic quotation and elevated words.
- Paragraphs are short (2-5 sentences) in the readers and one line each in the prophet stories.
- Vocabulary progresses from concrete household and nature nouns, through sira and school terms, to abstract moral and social terms (الفتنة، الإخلاص، الخشوع).

## 4. Tashkeel observed
- Prophet stories and reader part 1: full tashkeel on every word, including case endings (إِبْرَاهِيمُ، النَّاسُ، الْفَأْسَ) with tanween and shadda.
- Part 2: full or near-full tashkeel; the sira anecdotes are lightly voweled.
- Part 3: partial tashkeel, mostly on difficult or ambiguous words and the Quranic text. Lists of vocabulary are voweled on the headword.
- Punctuation uses spaces before commas and a full stop at the end of each line. Quranic verses are in ornate brackets.
- Ayat get full diacritics. Voweling the case endings (i'rab) consistently is a hallmark.

## 5. DO / DON'T
DO:
- Open with a simple event, time or place, and keep verbs in active, concrete form.
- Use "قال ... فقال ..." dialogue with direct speech and a colon.
- Name the moral implicitly through events, then pray or praise at the close.
- Match tashkeel density to the level.
- Use words a child of that level knows, and repeat key words.
DON'T:
- Use dialect, modern slang, English loanwords or abstract jargon at the lower levels.
- Write long subordinate chains in parts 1-2.
- Add suspense gimmicks, sarcasm or a modern emotional psychology.
- Describe faces or eyes of prophets or companions; keep to action and speech.
- Invent Quranic text; quote only verses you are sure of, or paraphrase without bracketing.
